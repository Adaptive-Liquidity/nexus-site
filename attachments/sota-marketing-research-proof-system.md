# Nexus-IQ SOTA Marketing, Research, Proof, and Demand System

## Purpose

This system turns Nexus-IQ’s technical substance into an institutional-quality public experience: precise enough for engineers, legible enough for executives, and compelling enough to earn continued attention.

The goal is not maximum hype. It is maximum qualified curiosity. The right reader should understand the consequential gap, inspect how the mechanism works, verify the evidence, and see why evaluating it now is more valuable than postponing the question.

This document is subordinate to the live repository, claims registry, product memory, maturity labels, limitations, and evidence artifacts. Never convert an aspiration into a present-tense capability.

## 1. Editorial constitution

Nexus-IQ’s strongest voice is direct, compressed, institutional, and mechanism-led.

Use:

- declarative sentences and concrete nouns and verbs;
- mechanism, sequence, receipts, and limitations before adjectives;
- contrast structures that expose category errors;
- exact maturity labels: current, experimental, in development, target, or proposed;
- visible definitions for overloaded terms;
- explicit “proves / does not prove” boundaries.

Preserve the established thesis:

> Consequential agent action belongs behind a commit boundary.

Preserve the established contrast:

> Intent is not authority. A tool call is not a transaction. A runtime report is not evidence.

Avoid unsupported superlatives, generic future-facing questions, invented adoption or endorsements, theatrical certainty, public-facing “FOMO” language, and fake scarcity. Use **earned urgency** internally: urgency created by a real change, real risk, real evaluation window, or real cost of inaction.

## 2. Audience architecture

Serve the same truth at different depths.

### Executive, security, and governance reader

Give them the decision or exposure, why existing controls are insufficient, the new boundary, what can be verified, current maturity, and the next evaluation step.

### Engineer, researcher, and evaluator

Give them definitions, assumptions, state transitions, threat model, interfaces, methods, samples, comparators, limitations, and reproducible artifacts.

### Investor, partner, analyst, or ecosystem reader

Give them the category, structural shift, mechanism-level differentiation, credible execution evidence, maturity and dependency map, and a defensible route to evaluation or partnership.

Every surface should let the reader descend to the next evidence level without losing context.

## 3. Message spine

Every major page or launch asset should answer these in order:

1. **Category tension** — Agents can initiate consequential actions, but most stacks do not provide a transaction-grade commit boundary.
2. **Consequence** — Intent can be confused with authority, execution with completion, and a report with proof.
3. **Mechanism** — Explain prepare, validate, commit or abort, and evidence.
4. **Proof** — Show a concrete, inspectable artifact or replay.
5. **Maturity** — State what is working, experimental, planned, or outside scope.
6. **Decision** — Offer a specific action: inspect, replay, compare, download, or request an evaluation.

The homepage creates the question. The mechanism resolves it. The evidence system lets the reader verify the resolution.

## 4. Proof ladder

Build claims upward. Never jump from assertion to authority styling.

1. Assertion — narrowly worded.
2. Definition — exact meaning, scope, and exclusions.
3. Mechanism — causal or procedural explanation.
4. Model — diagram, state machine, sequence, or interface.
5. Demonstration — interactive commit, abort, failure, or replay.
6. Evidence artifact — versioned output with provenance and integrity metadata.
7. Reproducible method — environment, procedure, sample, comparator, and expected result.
8. Independent validation — external review, replication, certification, or deployment evidence only when it exists.

For each proof object show what it proves, what it does not prove, version and date, environment and assumptions, owner or source, associated claim IDs, limitations, and known failure modes.

## 5. Claim and evidence registry

Use the repository’s claims registry as the public-communication control plane. If fields are missing, propose additions before changing the schema.

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Claim ID | Stable reference |
| Exact public wording | Prevents paraphrase drift |
| Claim type | Mechanism, performance, security, compatibility, adoption, or roadmap |
| Maturity | Current, experimental, in development, target, or proposed |
| Scope | Version, environment, use case, and exclusions |
| Evidence IDs | Direct supporting artifacts |
| Method | How evidence was produced |
| Sample and comparator | Denominator and baseline |
| Result | Value with unit and uncertainty |
| Limitations | What cannot be inferred |
| Owner and reviewer | Accountability |
| Last verified | Freshness |
| Review or expiry date | Prevents stale claims |

No public numerical claim should omit unit, denominator, conditions, comparator, and date.

## 6. Institutional research workflow

1. Frame the research question, decision, population or system scope, comparators, outcomes, and conflicts of interest.
2. Register the method before synthesis: repositories, complete search strings, date range, inclusion and exclusion rules, screening, quality rubric, extraction fields, synthesis, and protocol changes.
3. Prefer standards and primary documentation, peer-reviewed primary research, systematic reviews and meta-analyses, institutional reports, and versioned first-party artifacts. Record contradictory and null findings.
4. Build an evidence table with citation and identifier, source type, environment, method, sample, result, uncertainty, limitations, and linked claim IDs.
5. Distinguish observed from inferred, association from causation, local behavior from general behavior, and demonstrated capability from proposed architecture.
6. Publish research objects with metadata, persistent identifiers, provenance, versions, machine-readable formats, licenses, and citation instructions where security and licensing permit.

Use PRISMA 2020 only when the work genuinely qualifies as a systematic review. Label narrative and scoping work accurately. Apply FAIR principles to research objects without using FAIR as a claim of scientific validity.

## 7. Technical explanation patterns

### Boundary comparison

Show the same consequential action with and without a commit boundary. Keep the input constant so the difference is visible.

### State machine

Show permitted and invalid transitions, terminal states, timeouts, retries, aborts, and recovery. Do not animate only a happy path.

### Sequence

Separate principal, intent, authority, policy, executor, external effect, evidence artifact, and verifier. Make it obvious where authority is checked and the irreversible effect occurs.

### Threat model

Document protected assets, actors, attacker capabilities, assumptions, abuse cases, mitigations, residual risks, and out-of-scope threats.

### Proof Capsule

Each capsule contains the claim, mechanism, artifact, method, replay or inspection path, integrity and provenance data, limitations, and a “proves / does not prove” statement.

## 8. Progressive disclosure

Design five coherent depths:

1. **Ten seconds** — one consequential premise and one proof cue.
2. **Ninety seconds** — the category gap, transaction sequence, and inspectable outcome.
3. **Five to ten minutes** — interactive mechanism, commit and abort, evidence explorer, and maturity labels.
4. **Thirty minutes** — architecture, threat model, methods, benchmark protocol, limitations, and citations.
5. **Evaluator pack** — artifacts, reproduction steps, claim map, environment, changelog, contacts, and decision checklist.

Each layer should stand alone and invite the next layer.
## 9. Curiosity that closes the loop

Curiosity should reveal a precise missing piece, not withhold basic information.

1. State a familiar action.
2. Expose the consequential category error.
3. Give a partial mechanism immediately.
4. Put an inspectable proof object in view.
5. Let the reader predict or choose what happens.
6. Resolve the question through interaction.
7. Offer deeper evidence.

Example:

> The agent says the transfer succeeded. Which event made it authoritative?

Show the candidate events, then let the reader inspect why intent, a tool call, and a runtime report are insufficient before reaching the commit artifact.

A 2025 registered report across 8,977 headline experiments found a curved relationship between headline concreteness and selection: both excessive vagueness and excessive disclosure can underperform. Apply that result cautiously—give enough specificity to make the gap consequential, while leaving a real, answerable mechanism to inspect.

Every open loop must close on the same page or a clearly named destination. Never manufacture confusion solely to raise clicks.

## 10. Interaction as explanation

Prioritize interactions that teach or verify:

- scenario selector for consequential actions;
- commit versus abort decision;
- capability and policy inspection;
- transaction replay;
- evidence artifact explorer;
- claim-to-evidence map;
- maturity and limitations matrix;
- benchmark explorer with method, sample, comparator, and caveats;
- threat-model overlay;
- “what would you permit?” capability builder;
- evaluator mode with restrained motion and dense technical detail.

Visuals alone do not guarantee learning. A 2020 systematic review of 44 studies found that visual displays require appropriate interaction and instructional support. Every cinematic or diagrammatic element therefore needs a defined explanatory task, label, and accessible alternative.

## 11. Earned urgency and ethical demand

Create urgency only from facts: a documented increase in consequential autonomy, real governance exposure, dated evaluation or standards window, current opportunity to reproduce evidence, or measurable cost of delaying an architectural decision.

Never use fake countdowns, false scarcity, fabricated activity, preselected consent, disguised advertising, buried limitations, or invented customer logos and testimonials. The FTC identifies fake countdowns, false scarcity, and hidden terms as deceptive dark patterns. Nexus-IQ should do the opposite: material information becomes more visible as commitment increases.

Uncertainty is not automatically a credibility loss. A 2026 meta-analysis of 28 studies found no overall systematic effect of uncertainty communication on trust. State uncertainty precisely and pair it with method, decision relevance, and the next verification step.

### Homepage

- consequential premise;
- Intent → Gap → Execute → commit or abort → Evidence;
- a versioned proof cue;
- current maturity and limitations;
- replay, architecture, research, and evaluation actions.

### Mechanism

- transaction state machine;
- authority and capability model;
- failure and recovery semantics;

### Evidence

- claim registry and Proof Capsules;
- evaluation methods;
- inspectable artifacts and reproduction instructions;
- provenance and integrity;
- negative and null results;
- changelog.

### Research library

- standards and peer-reviewed sources;
- Nexus-IQ papers and technical notes;
- methods and search logs;
- citation exports;

### Evaluate

- evaluator questions;
- environment and integration requirements;
- threat-model worksheet;
- evidence request;
- technical contact path;
- no false implication of availability, access, or support.

## 13. Search and discovery

Build for people first, then make the truth machine-readable. For substantive pages expose the responsible author or team, why the content exists, how research or media was produced, sources, artifact links, dates, maturity, limitations, and change history.

Develop topic clusters around genuine expertise: transactional agents, proof-carrying execution, capability security, commit and rollback semantics, governed memory, agent evidence and replay, and evaluation methods for consequential agent action.

Use structured data only when the visible page supports every property. Relevant types may include Organization, SoftwareApplication, TechArticle, and Dataset. Do not invent ratings, offers, authors, dates, or datasets. Prefer JSON-LD, follow Google policies, and validate rendered output.

## 14. Distribution system

Create one evidence-bearing anchor asset, then adapt—without changing claim scope—by channel.

Anchor assets include the launch thesis, a technical paper, interactive transaction demo, evaluation release, and evidence pack.

- **X:** one category error, one mechanism visual, one proof link.
- **LinkedIn:** decision context, consequence, mechanism, maturity, evaluator action.
- **Video:** commit and abort paths, captions, transcript, and sources.
- **Newsletter:** what changed, new evidence, limitations, and evaluator action.
- **GitHub:** reproducible method, versioned artifacts, issues, and support boundaries.
- **Technical communities:** mechanism-first explanation and candid request for critique.
- **Press or analyst brief:** category, evidence map, spokesperson, limitations, and source pack.

No automated posting, paid promotion, outreach, or external publishing without explicit authorization.

## 15. Measurement and experimentation

The north star is qualified evaluation intent supported by proof engagement, not page views alone.

Measure demo starts and completions, commit and abort paths, artifact inspection, claim and limitation views, method and citation expansion, evaluator-pack downloads, technical depth, qualified evaluation requests, and return visits to updated evidence.

For experiments: state the hypothesis; define the primary metric and guardrails; set audience and duration; prevent conflicting tests; include accessibility and trust guardrails; record null results; and stop tests that confuse maturity, evidence, privacy, or availability.

Performance is part of persuasion. Evaluate Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1. These are field goals, not guarantees from one lab run.

## 16. Tools and authorization boundaries

### Research and evidence

Zotero; Crossref; OpenAlex; Semantic Scholar; scholarly indexes; DOI and archives; GitHub Releases and checksums; signed attestations, SBOMs, Sigstore, or SLSA-aligned provenance when applicable; repository claim registry and evidence manifest.

### Editorial and QA

Vale or equivalent; markdownlint; link and DOI checking; WCAG 2.2 review; Playwright; Lighthouse and field monitoring; structured-data validation.

### Visual and analytical

Mermaid or Figma for architecture; Observable Plot, D3, or accessible charts only when data needs them; reduced-motion and static alternatives for explanatory animation.

### Analytics and experimentation

PostHog, Plausible, GrowthBook, Search Console, or similar services are optional, not defaults. Inspect the current stack, privacy terms, consent, cost, and authorization before adding any external service. Never place secrets in prompts, source, screenshots, events, or client bundles.

## 17. Agent and editorial roles

When multiple agents or people are available, give them non-overlapping ownership:

- Research Lead — method, search log, evidence table, synthesis.
- Technical SME — mechanism and architecture accuracy.
- Claims Reviewer — wording, maturity, provenance, limitations.
- Skeptical Reviewer — disconfirming evidence and overclaiming.
- Narrative Editor — progressive disclosure and voice.
- Interaction Designer — explanatory interaction and accessibility.
- Search and Distribution Editor — discovery and adaptation.
- QA Lead — links, citations, performance, routes, accessibility, regressions.

The lead integrator owns the final synthesis. Parallel work does not authorize parallel edits to the same shared file.

## 18. Acceptance gates

Do not call the content system complete until:

- every material claim maps to evidence or is labeled hypothesis, target, or proposal;
- numerical claims include method, unit, denominator, comparator, conditions, and date;
- contradictory and null evidence is represented;
- major technical visuals have labels, a purpose, and accessible alternatives;
- every curiosity loop resolves into mechanism or evidence;
- no fake scarcity, fabricated social proof, or dark pattern exists;
- maturity states remain distinct;
- methods and limitations are reachable without hidden UI;
- responsible ownership and review date are visible;
- citations, identifiers, links, structured data, and artifacts validate;
- critical flows pass keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, responsive, and browser checks;
- performance is measured and reported honestly;
- publishing, analytics, uploads, paid tools, outreach, deployment, and migrations remain separately authorized.
